## What changed

The admin chrome comes down from three sticky rows to two, a member can renew their own membership without asking an admin, the app stops explaining controls that already explain themselves, and a tournament played one against one stops being described as a set of teams.

The admin chrome is two rows and the two levels of it look different. The association mark, the sections and the way out used to hold a row of their own above the sections strip, and on the money screens a third strip of pill buttons sat under that, repeating the treatment of the strip directly above it so the two read as one block and neither said which level it was. The mark, the sections and the way out now share one row, so every admin screen is one row of chrome and the money screens are two. The screens inside a section are an underlined rail rather than a second set of pills, and they drop their icons, two of which were the same drawing on different screens. The panel name shows where there is room for it and the way out keeps a name a screen reader can read whether or not its label is on screen. The payments list starts about forty pixels higher than it did.

A member whose membership covers an earlier year can renew it themselves. The card on home and on the profile used to say the association was collecting a later year and send the reader to the association, and it now offers the renewal, keeping the old sentence only for a member with no membership number, which is the one case a renewal is refused. The form is the one that takes a first membership, with the same method, account, amount, reference and proof, and it asks the member to write their membership number in the reason for the transfer where a first membership asks for the order code.

What a member's renewal writes is not what an admin's writes. An admin recording a renewal has seen the money, so that row is accepted with the admin named as its reviewer. A member has not been reviewed by anyone, so the row waits for review the way a first membership does, with no reviewer, no review date, and the member named as the one who recorded it. It reaches the review queue beside a first membership, an admin accepts or refuses it there with the same two buttons, accepting it does not issue a second membership number, and the member stays named as the one who recorded it after an admin has passed it. A member with a renewal already waiting cannot open another, and is told the review is what is holding it rather than that their membership was never accepted, which is the reason that used to be given.

The app stops saying what its controls already say. A survey of one pattern across the admin side found the same shape in a dozen places, a label saying what a thing is, a sentence under it saying the same in more words, and sometimes a button saying it a third time. The support privacy card named its setting in a heading and again on its checkbox. The association settings carried a sentence under six of seven fields, two of which restated the label and one of which was printed twice under two adjacent fields. The bracket card named a random knockout draw in a heading, in a hint and on the button. The tournament and volunteer campaign cards each opened their hint with the button under it. The tournament setup wizard explained direct elimination as one loss and out. The quiz question settings stacked two paragraphs between a toggle and the fields the second one described. What survives in each of these is only what a label cannot carry, and what went is gone from the text modules rather than left unused.

Some of those controls were the wrong shape rather than the wrong words. The member page's two permanent deletions were a heading, a sentence, a button, a second sentence and a second button, and they are now two buttons, with each sentence moved into the confirmation, which now says which of the two deletions is about to happen instead of describing the other one. The photo card was a heading, a state line and two notes above two buttons, and is now two buttons with a badge that appears only while the lock is on, and deleting a picture is confirmed, as is locking one, because locking deletes the picture that is there. The review queue taught its keyboard shortcuts in a sentence above the buttons, on every phone, and listed half of them, and the two that have a control now sit on that control and only where a keyboard is likely. The member drawer reached the same page twice, once as a small pill labelled as an edit and once as a bare line of mint text, and now offers it once, as a control, labelled with where it goes. The two upload controls named their own buttons in a line above them, and the picture frame told the reader to click a picture whose whole job is to be clicked, which it already said with the camera badge across its foot. The group draw taught a swap in a sentence because its chips were flat pale rows with no border and no icon, and the chips now carry both. The form that creates an admin account described, in three lines, a scoping the role select does not offer, and the picker that does the scoping now names the permission it is about to hand out instead of letting it happen quietly.

A tournament played one against one stops being described as a set of teams. The screen that creates a tournament never said such a tournament was possible, since it is a squad fixed at one player and that convention was written nowhere, and it now offers the choice directly, beside the squad heading so it costs no row, and hides the squad fields and the word team once it is on. Nothing new is stored. Singles stays derived from the squad range, read through the same helper as the rest of the app.

The registrants tab of such a tournament offered a filter with one chip per registrant, each labelled with a person's name, above a list of those same people, because the panel drew the chips on there being teams at all and never asked what kind of tournament it was. That filter is gone there, the search stops offering to look inside a team, and each person is named once instead of once as themselves and once as the team named after them. A tournament with real teams keeps all of it.

Under that screen, the registrants and the players were two lists that could hold different answers about the same people. A registration became an entrant only at the moment it was made or accepted, and nothing re-ran that when an activity later became a tournament or its squad was set to one, so a tournament settled after people had registered believed it had no players and offered to add them one at a time. In a tournament of one against one the registrants are the players, so there is one list, and seating is reconciled rather than only created. Saving a tournament's shape now seats every accepted registration that has no seat, and clears the seats it made when the shape stops being one against one, keeping any that has been given a name of its own, has gained a second member, or has already played. The tournaments already in that state are repaired by opening their setup and saving it.

The scorers and cards toggle is offered only where it does something. On a tournament whose matches are a series it was not merely misplaced. The result screen wraps the whole scorers, cards and man of the match block in football and reads the toggle only inside it, so there it was stored, echoed, copied and logged, and controlled nothing that can render.

One activity photo is rendered in four shapes and they no longer disagree about what to throw away. The wide frame in the details form cropped to a short strip, so a photograph of a game in progress became a band in which the game was not visible, while the member page showed the same file whole over a blurred copy of itself. The two large frames now share that treatment and that shape, so an admin uploading a picture sees what a member will see, and the same change reaches the three payment dialogs that use the same frame, where a tall receipt was being sliced through the middle. The small square beside a title and the thumbnail in a row keep their crop, which is what reads at that size.

## Why

Three rows of chrome is a quarter of a phone screen spent before the first row of a list, and a strip that looks exactly like the strip above it is not a second level, it is the same level drawn twice.

Renewal was admin only, and everything a member's renewal needs already existed and was not reachable from the member side. The one part that had to be built rather than reused is the part that decides whether a member can mark themselves paid, which is why the status of the row is the first thing the tests hold still.

The prose is one habit rather than a dozen faults. A control that has to be explained is usually a control that is the wrong shape, and the explanations were hiding that. Where the explanation was only noise it went, and where it was standing in for an affordance the affordance was built and then it went.

The tournament of one against one has worked since the last release and the app had never learned to talk about it. The registrant who is not a player, the filter with a chip per person and the setup screen that cannot say what it is making are the same gap in three places, and the seating that ran once at registration time is what let a deployed tournament end up believing nobody had entered it.

## How it was tested

Every pull request ran the tier that covers what it changed before it was opened.

The renewal was driven over the wire rather than through the screen. A member whose membership covers an earlier year opens the running year and the row comes out waiting for review, with no reviewer and no review date and the member named as the one who recorded it. The money lands as a payment on the new year waiting on the same review, and the year already paid keeps its own row and its own payment. Each reason a renewal is refused returns its own message, an amount under the fee and a missing proof are refused, a method members are not offered is refused, and a request with no session is turned away. A member who tries twice ends with one row for the year and one payment holding the first amount. The queue and the review were driven the same way, from the row standing beside a first membership through to acceptance, refusal, and amending a refused renewal on the row it already has.

The seating was driven through the route that changes a tournament's shape. A tournament that becomes one against one seats the accepted registrations that predate the squad, a plain activity that becomes one does the same, a registration that was never accepted stays unseated, running it twice seats nobody twice, and each seat carries the name of the person it holds. Going the other way clears those seats and keeps a seat that was renamed, one that gained a second member and one that has played.

The deployed tournaments were read and not written. A check across every match found none pointing at an entrant that does not exist, so nothing that has been played needs unpicking and the tournaments in that state are repaired by saving their setup rather than by a script.

The screens were read at 360 pixels, which no tier covers. The payments screen before and after, and a section with no screens under it. The card on home for a member who can renew and one who cannot. The danger card, the photo card, the settings form, the quiz question card, the group draw, the tournament setup with and without one against one, the registrants tab of both kinds of tournament, the member drawer header with a long name, and both upload controls. The four frames an activity photo lands in were read together against a tall picture, which is the shape a wide crop destroys.
